## The story to preserve

One illustrative supplier assessment, not a customer claim: an operations lead delegates document research; the agent prepares a recommendation; an expired certificate triggers review; an authorised person accepts, requests better evidence or rejects. Acceptance is of the recommendation, not purchase authority.

The application enforces scope and holds actions requiring approval. Orvessian's intended receipt/review layer records the relevant evidence and decisions; it does not itself imply a complete enforcement integration is live.

## Website destinations

- `/in-action` and compatibility `/use-cases`: brief, work, exception and review chapters; three visible alternative outcomes; changed/new/missing evidence cases; an illustrative decision package; private/public boundaries.
- `/product`: receipt, review and network layers; shared boundary explanation; accountable application, organisation and verifier roles.
- `/vision`: human purpose; enterprise workflows first, cross-organisation work next, physical systems later; evidence gates for each expansion.
- Homepage and primary navigation: direct links into the expanded story and Vision page.

## Content invariants for the later slideshow

1. All scenario data remains labelled illustrative.
2. The three decision paths are alternatives, not simultaneous outcomes.
3. A new evidence version requires fresh review; earlier decisions remain in history.
4. A mismatched disclosure fails its integrity check; absent evidence stays unavailable.
5. Matching a commitment does not prove evidence is true.
6. The decision package is a human-readable product concept, not the canonical receipt schema.
7. No cryptographic or chain checks are claimed to run in the website illustration.
8. Silence is not approval in this proposed workflow. A timeout keeps acceptance on hold.
9. Private records, authorised disclosures and public anchors have different visibility; metadata and disclosed copies retain privacy risks.
10. Robotics remains a future integration direction, not a safety claim.

## Phase 3 asset brief inputs

Use the same brief → evidence → exception → review → record sequence for the Living Receipt. Animate only what helps explain those relationships. Preserve direct chapter links, readable content, failure cases and the static/reduced-motion experience. Do not turn the story into pretend approve/reject controls that look operational.

## Verification scope

Phase 2 uses a production build and a repeatable local route/content/anchor smoke check. These establish rendering and expected story coverage; they are not a moderated audience-comprehension study or full cross-device visual/accessibility review. Those remain later validation work.
