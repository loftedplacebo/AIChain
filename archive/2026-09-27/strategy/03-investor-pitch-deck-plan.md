## 1. What this deck must sell

**Thesis:** if organisations delegate more work to autonomous systems, they will need evidence and accountability that travel across those systems. Orvessian is developing the verification layer for that work, beginning with enterprise agent workflows and expanding toward machine activity.

The investment argument must connect a large future to a narrow initial customer problem, a credible technical foundation, an achievable route to adoption and milestones that funding can unlock. The chain and token are not substitutes for customer demand.

Use approximately 14 core slides for a live conversation, plus a diligence appendix. A send-ahead PDF can include short explanatory captions but should not become the whitepaper. Both versions should work without animation.

The structure adapts the company purpose, problem, solution, timing, market, alternatives, business model, team and vision questions in [Sequoia's pitch guidance](https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan). The Orvessian narrative and slide recommendations below are our own.

## 2. Core slides

### 1 — Autonomous agents. Human certainty.

**Takeaway:** Orvessian is building the verification layer for autonomous work.

Use one clear sentence beneath the headline and the signature layered-receipt render. Show founder name and verified contact details when supplied. “Internal alpha” can be a small status label.

**Evidence needed:** confirmed brand/operator identity and presentation date. No token ticker or market-size claim on the cover.

### 2 — The work moves forward. The evidence stays fragmented.

**Takeaway:** someone remains responsible when an agent acts across tools and organisations.

Describe the buyer's current reconstruction problem: what was authorised, which evidence was used, who approved the exception and whether the record changed. Show one real workflow or an explicitly illustrative one.

**Visual:** an agent job crossing systems, with the reviewer's unanswered questions beside it.

**Evidence needed:** customer interviews, current process, review effort and examples of hand-off failure. Until obtained, label the pain as a hypothesis; do not invent a cost-of-failure statistic.

### 3 — More autonomy creates a new accountability requirement.

**Takeaway:** the proposed timing advantage comes from software taking action, not merely producing text.

Explain why platform-local logs and manual reconstruction may become insufficient for cross-party workflows. The public developer platforms researched for the website already present agent infrastructure; this is a directional signal, not market sizing. Validate the urgency with buyers' actual deployment plans. [Vercel](https://vercel.com/), [Temporal](https://temporal.io/)

**Visual:** progression from assistant to delegated workflow to cross-organisation work; robotics appears as a later horizon.

**Evidence needed:** current, sourced adoption evidence relevant to the selected buyer, procurement timing and unmet requirements.

### 4 — Give autonomous work an inspectable record.

**Takeaway:** connect authority, evidence and checks in portable verification receipts.

Explain the product in three plain verbs: record, review, verify. Identify who uses it and where it sits in an existing workflow. Separate working receipt infrastructure from the proposed end-user Review experience.

**Visual:** the receipt exploded into labelled components. Keep “what this proves” distinct from “what the issuer claims”.

**Evidence needed:** actual sample receipt and current capability labels.

### 5 — One job, from brief to accountable outcome.

**Takeaway:** the product has a recognisable day-to-day use.

Walk through supplier assessment: approved sources, evidence collection, expired-document exception, human review, signed record and selective disclosure. Show a rejection or changed-evidence case too.

**Visual:** six simple stills from the website's guided story. A short demo is optional; retain a static fallback.

**Evidence needed:** clear distinction between the synthetic story, implemented alpha operations and proposed workflow integrations. Do not call a concept animation a live demo.

### 6 — The technical foundation is already taking shape.

**Takeaway:** there is a concrete alpha to inspect, with explicit limits.

Show three evidence cards: receipt tooling; bounded authority/proof/anchor integration; recovery and lookup evidence. Link the exact reviewed repository version and reports when the deck is authored.

**Visual:** a compact implemented / demonstrated / next table. No unqualified throughput graph.

**Evidence needed:** the [integrated alpha report](C:/AIChain/docs/archive/2026-09-27/completed-stages/phase-3-integrated-alpha.md) and [receipt readiness review](C:/AIChain/docs/verification-receipt-readiness-review.md), independently checked before external circulation. State synthetic workloads, local SDK status and absent production/security validation.

### 7 — Start where the evidence trail already matters.

**Takeaway:** the initial customer is a team deploying consequential enterprise agent workflows.

Name the champion, budget owner and first integration surface. Proposed entry: an engineering-led pilot with an AI/platform operations owner, involving audit/security reviewers. Start with a bounded supplier-assessment or similar evidence-heavy workflow selected through interviews.

**Visual:** one customer profile and one workflow, not a grid of ten industries.

**Evidence needed:** interviews, design-partner pipeline, willingness to pay, pilot scope and success criteria. Label signed pilots, exploratory conversations and target accounts separately.

### 8 — A focused entry into a broader infrastructure opportunity.

**Takeaway:** a narrow workflow can expand into a reusable verification layer.

Size the initial market from target organisations and plausible annual contract value; validate both. Show a separate expansion scenario for additional workflows, platforms and eventual machine evidence.

**Visual:** bottom-up calculation and staged expansion, not a giant “AI market” circle.

**Evidence needed:** sourced organisation counts, reachable segments, procurement assumptions, pricing interviews and low/base/high scenarios. Distinguish serviceable market from the share the team can realistically win. Do not multiply all AI actions by a hypothetical fee and present the result as revenue.

### 9 — The opportunity sits between execution and independent review.

**Takeaway:** Orvessian complements existing tools but must beat credible alternatives.

Compare the customer's options: build a signed audit log internally; use agent/runtime observability; extend governance tools; combine identity/payment protocols; use existing-chain anchoring. Assess portability, private disclosure, authority history, verification semantics, operations and integration burden.

**Visual:** a small evidence-backed comparison table; mark unresearched cells unknown rather than giving Orvessian every green tick.

**Evidence needed:** current primary product documentation, buyer comparisons and cost of substitution. Defensibility is a hypothesis around interoperability, operational quality and trusted integrations—not the word “decentralised”.

### 10 — Build adoption through one useful integration.

**Takeaway:** the route to market starts with developers solving a buyer's concrete problem.

Proposed sequence: recruit design partners → support a scoped integration → measure reconstruction/review benefits → obtain repeat use → package the common workflow → expand through agent/runtime partners.

**Visual:** adoption sequence with measurable gates rather than a self-reinforcing flywheel assumed from day one.

**Evidence needed:** acquisition channels, integration duration, partner commitments, pilot conversion and reasons for non-adoption. No partner logos without permission and a real relationship.

### 11 — Monetise the service, not activity for its own sake.

**Takeaway:** company value needs a business model independent of token speculation.

Proposed commercial model: managed evidence/verification infrastructure, organisation review features and enterprise support. Keep open receipt tooling useful as an adoption channel. Test subscription plus usage pricing against predictable customer budgets; do not announce prices before validation.

**Visual:** customer pays for service → costs for storage, verification, support and network interaction → company contribution margin. Separate network fees and any future token mechanism.

**Evidence needed:** willingness to pay and a model including verification cost, proof cost, human review, storage/retention, integration support and chain costs. Buyer fees, provider payments, network rewards and company revenue must not be double-counted.

### 12 — Why this team can earn the right to build it.

**Takeaway:** founder insight and execution fit the next phase.

Present real experience, relevant work shipped, independent collaborators and skills still needed. Connect the technical progress to actual ownership without implying an audit or external endorsement.

**Visual:** real founder/team portraits with brief relevant credentials. No generated team photographs.

**Evidence needed:** founder biographies, employment/achievement confirmation and advisor consent. Team information has not been supplied for this plan; the slide is a factual-content requirement, not an invitation to invent a founding team.

### 13 — Funding buys measurable de-risking.

**Takeaway:** the next round should fund evidence of adoption and readiness.

Proposed milestones: an independent developer completes the full evidence lifecycle; durable evidence operations pass recovery tests; verifier semantics and stable SDK packaging receive external review; design partners demonstrate repeat workflow use; approved closed-testnet operations produce their own evidence.

**Visual:** milestones with owner, acceptance evidence and budget category. Use dates only after capacity planning.

**Evidence needed:** funding amount, instrument, runway, existing commitments, hiring plan and costed milestones. Show placeholders in the internal draft as **[founder input required]** and remove unresolved figures before circulation. Do not infer that investment includes token rights.

### 14 — Autonomy with an accountable history.

**Takeaway:** the near-term product is specific; the long-term ambition is substantial.

Return to the human purpose. Begin with enterprise agents; extend the verification foundation across organisations and eventually physical machines as integration and assurance mature. End with the exact investment ask once approved and a real contact route.

**Visual:** the original receipt motif connected to a restrained software-to-machine horizon. Do not introduce a new product proposition on the final slide.

## 3. Diligence appendix

Prepare modular slides on:

1. Why an independent L1 versus an existing chain or transparency service; security and operating trade-offs.
2. Receipt architecture, trust boundaries and assurance semantics.
3. Current alpha versus proposed product capabilities.
4. Reproduction evidence and benchmark methodology, including negative results.
5. Evidence privacy, retention, disclosure and availability.
6. Verifier trust, human-review quality, revocation and disputes.
7. Competitive research with dated primary sources.
8. Bottom-up market model and pricing assumptions.
9. Unit economics and sensitivity to human-review/proof costs.
10. Pilot funnel and results, when real.
11. Network economics research, supply/reward decisions and unresolved attack incentives.
12. Company structure, IP ownership, cap table and financing materials for appropriate controlled disclosure.

The legal/financing materials require founder and appropriate specialist input. This planning document does not provide legal or investment advice.

## 4. Asset reuse without making every channel identical

Reuse the homepage's receipt object on the cover and solution slide; the In action scenario on the demo slide; the technical boundary diagram in the appendix; the status evidence in the progress slide. All should have a static export.

The website invites exploration. The paper enables scrutiny. The deck makes an investment argument. Do not convert the homepage into fourteen screenshots or copy the whitepaper into slide-sized text.

## 5. Readiness checklist

- One-sentence proposition is understandable without blockchain knowledge.
- A specific buyer and pain precede market size and token economics.
- Every metric has a source, date, definition and owner.
- Every concept demo and future capability is labelled.
- Customer and partner claims reflect actual relationships.
- The chain's purpose, alternatives and security burden are addressed.
- Team, funding ask, runway and milestone budget are supplied and reconciled.
- The spoken deck works in a concise meeting; the send-ahead version works without narration.

**Most important missing inputs:** buyer evidence, team credentials, commercial model validation and a costed funding ask. Better visual design can make these arguments clearer; it cannot substitute for them.
