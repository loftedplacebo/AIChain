14 September 2026 · Proposed programme · Owner role: founder with platform/DevRel support

## Aim and cohort design

Recruit towards 10–15 independent AI companies, beginning with 3 closely supported design partners. The programme tests whether independent evidence solves a real customer problem. It should produce reusable integration patterns, reviewer feedback and repeat use rather than a logo collection.

Initial capacity assumption: onboard one new partner per week after P1, with a capped engineering allowance. Expand only when existing partners can operate without continual intervention. No outreach, customer contact or grant award has been performed in this review.

## Qualification

Score each dimension 0–2: recurring evidence pain; named evidence recipient; engineering access; existing AI workflow; decision-maker access; ability to trial low-sensitivity data; repeat volume; willingness to discuss payment. Prioritise scores of 12/16 or more with nonzero evidence pain, recipient and engineering ownership. A score is an internal heuristic, not objective market validation.

Exclude teams seeking token incentives without a workflow, speculative deployments with no owner, and projects requiring a regulated production guarantee the platform cannot yet supply. Keep declined reasons to improve targeting. Do not demand public endorsement as a condition of receiving technical support.

## Discovery script

Ask for a recent concrete incident: what action needed explanation, who asked, which logs/documents were gathered, how long it took, what could not be independently checked, and what consequences followed. Establish frequency, current cost, sensitivity/retention, existing trace stack, budget owner and procurement route.

Then show the scoped receipt/export example. Ask the recipient to explain what they believe it proves. Correct misconceptions before asking about willingness to pay. The key question is whether an independently checked record changes an actual decision, not whether the cryptography sounds attractive.

## Pilot charter template

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Organisation / owners | Private CRM identifier, integration owner, reviewer and commercial sponsor |
| Workflow | One named event and its real evidence recipient |
| Baseline | Current retrieval/review process and measured time/cost |
| Receipt policy | Event definition, profile, signing identity, selected checks and confirmation policy |
| Environment | Private testnet/public testnet/production, reset and assurance limitations |
| Data responsibilities | Customer-held bytes/openings by default; retention and permitted support access |
| Trial scope | Four-week repeat-use period after onboarding, with capped assistance and credits |
| Success | First event, independent export check, repeat usage, reviewer usefulness and cost evidence |
| Commercial review | Proposed conversion date, budget owner and paid-service requirements |
| Publication | Separate opt-in for name/logo/case study; no implied permission |
| Exit | Export, account/key revocation, deletion/retention handling, feedback |

Professional review should turn this into appropriate pilot terms before use; this template is not an executed contract.

## Offer and constraints

Provide time-limited API credits and integration assistance, access to roadmap discussions, and support for one selected workflow. Set a written allowance, such as a planning maximum of two engineer-days per partner plus weekly office hours, then adjust from real experience. Do not promise unlimited support or permanent free usage.

Treat grants separately: defined deliverable, maintainer/license requirements, completion evidence and budget owner. Prefer improvements that become reusable adapters, examples or verification tooling. Token grants are not part of the initial offer while token arrangements remain unresolved.

## Journey and success measures

| Stage | Evidence to record | Failure/next action |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Real workflow, reviewer and budget hypotheses | No recurring need: decline or change segment |
| Qualified | Named integration owner and scoped trial | No owner: do not spend integration capacity |
| Activated | SDK-created event independently checked | Document exact onboarding friction and repair it |
| Repeating | Qualifying use in four consecutive weekly cohorts | Distinguish product failure, one-off need and lack of demand |
| Valuable | Recipient verifies an export; before/after task measurement | Anchoring adds no value: investigate alternative offer |
| Paid | Signed paid scope and actual collection/usage | Pricing/procurement blocker recorded, not hidden |
| Reference | Explicit approved case-study content | Keep identity private without permission |

Track the funnel from qualified conversation to activated integration to repeated use to paid conversion. Partner count means independent organisations, not API keys, developers, subsidiaries counted twice, or letters of intent alone.

## First 90-day commercial cadence

Weeks 1–2: ten interviews and qualification of first three partners. Weeks 3–5: private demonstrations and charter agreement alongside P1. Weeks 6–9: supported pilots, weekly technical feedback, first price conversations. Weeks 10–13: repeat-use/retention review, paid offer decisions and expansion towards 10–15 teams only if onboarding capacity and release gates permit.

These are planning windows. Public onboarding remains blocked by unmet network/service release gates, even when a partner wishes to move faster. Private discovery and synthetic demonstrations can continue.

## Case study outline

Describe the workflow, original evidence problem, integration effort, what was captured, exact checks performed, network environment, independently verified export, measured reviewer benefit and remaining limits. Include metrics only for a specified period with customer approval. Do not turn a pilot into a claim of certified compliance, model correctness or production-scale adoption.
