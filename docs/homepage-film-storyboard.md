# Homepage film storyboard

## Concept

**One action. Different views. One trusted record.**

A short, human-first film about an AI customer-service assistant handling a missing-delivery refund. The story explains Orvessian through people who need the result: the customer, the support lead and the later reviewer.

The current web animatic uses six clear steps: missing delivery, permitted checks, approval boundary, daytime human review, customer resolution and the shared record. The first and resolution scenes deliberately show an empty entryway: the issue is a delivery that has not arrived.

## Recommended format

- **Homepage loop:** 12–15 seconds, silent, designed to loop after the hero copy.
- **Full explainer:** 45–60 seconds, optional sound, captions and a clear play control.
- **Style:** cinematic hybrid. Human, natural footage is primary; restrained amber-glass record animation connects the story. Avoid crypto imagery, robot characters, floating code and dense interface text.

## Storyboard: full explainer

| Time | Story beat | Visual | On-screen copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5s | The human problem | A customer at home sees that a delivery has not arrived and opens support chat. | “AI is moving from answers to actions.” |
| 5–12s | Useful work | The assistant checks the order, delivery signal and policy context. Keep the interface implied rather than readable. | “It can help—within clear limits.” |
| 12–20s | The exception | The requested refund crosses the assistant’s approved limit. The action pauses; an amber control signal becomes visible. | “When a limit is crossed, the workflow pauses.” |
| 20–30s | Human judgement | A support lead reviews the relevant context and approves the refund. | “A person makes the consequential decision.” |
| 30–40s | Different views | The customer sees a simple resolution. The support lead sees the authority and case context. An auditor later sees the connected timeline. | “One action. Different views.” |
| 40–52s | The trusted record | Authority, exception and approval resolve into the amber glass record. Show three views connecting to one underlying history. | “One trusted record.” |
| 52–60s | Brand close | The record settles into the Orvessian mark and the site’s glass language. | “Autonomy needs a trusted record.” |

## Homepage loop

Do not try to narrate the whole scenario. Start at the exception and move through the three essential beats:

1. A routine action advances.
2. An amber control event pauses it.
3. A human approval causes the record to settle into one connected glass history.

The website supplies the explanatory copy beside or over the loop. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; provide a still poster and never autoplay sound.

## What each person sees

| Person | Their useful view |
|---|---|
| Customer | The outcome and a simple explanation of what was checked. |
| Support lead | The authority, exception and evidence needed to make the decision. |
| Auditor | A scoped, reviewable timeline of the authority, action, alert and approval. |

Every view must respect access rights. “Independent record” means the selected history is harder for one operator to quietly rewrite; it does not disclose all private data, prove every source event true, or replace real-time controls.

## Production notes

- Generate or film each shot separately; use the same casting, palette and lighting treatment across shots.
- Add short text as web or edit overlays, not as generated footage.
- Keep interface details abstract and legible only where the story needs them.
- Use sound design sparingly: message arrival, a soft pause pulse, a warm confirmation and a glass-like resolve.
- Create captions and a transcript before publication.
