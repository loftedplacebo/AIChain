# Orvessian commercial model

**Status:** pre-testnet product planning · **Last updated:** 19 September 2026

## Position

Orvessian is intended to be open, independent trust infrastructure for autonomous systems, with commercial SDK/API and evidence products above the network. The commercial layer should help organisations govern, safeguard and account for consequential AI actions without turning protocol economics into a speculative product story.

The customer value is practical: make selected actions, context, checks and exceptions easier for authorised people to review after the event. It does not promise that an AI decision was correct, replace application controls, or eliminate human responsibility.

## Commercial principles

1. **Sell useful software and support.** Revenue should come from implementation, workspace access, developer tooling and measured service—not from a token thesis.
2. **Keep network costs visible.** Any future gas or anchoring cost should be separately visible and attributable, rather than buried in a platform margin.
3. **Price the logical record, not an imaginary transaction.** The architecture uses validation, batching, proofs and indexing. A customer-facing event may not map one-to-one to a chain transaction.
4. **Validate before committing.** No final pricing, service level, public testnet access or production offer should be promised before the relevant operational and economic evidence exists.
5. **Preserve buyer choice.** Deployment may ultimately include customer-operated, hosted and consortium models; this remains a commercial and deployment decision.

## Indicative pre-testnet offer design

These ranges are planning assumptions for discovery and design. They are not a current offer, price commitment or production price list.

| Offer | Intended customer need | Indicative planning range | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Design partner pilot | Establish whether one consequential workflow benefits from independent records | £15,000–£30,000 fixed, 8–12 weeks | Includes workflow mapping, authority/exception design, evidence model and a measured prototype. No production SLA. |
| Trust workspace | Review cases, policies, authorities and linked records | £2,000–£5,000 per organisation/month | Planned post-testnet subscription. Scope, users, retention and support will be set from pilot evidence. |
| Independent record API | Connect applications to receipt, batching, proof and lookup capabilities | £0.05–£0.25 per recorded event | Illustrative platform usage band only. It excludes any future, separately displayed network cost and must be recalibrated after testnet measurement. |

## What must be proven before testnet pricing is reviewed

- The cost of durable evidence, batch construction, submission, indexing, proof retrieval and support under realistic load.
- Testnet confirmation behaviour, queue limits, backpressure and recovery across multiple hosts.
- The customer value of reduced review effort, faster exception handling and more credible post-event explanation.
- A sustainable fee policy: block interval, gas/minimum fee, priority-fee treatment, byte pricing, quotas and anti-spam controls.
- Deployment and governance requirements for customer-operated, hosted or possible consortium arrangements.

## Protocol and product boundary

Current phase documentation authorises only a closed-testnet foundation with valueless disposable test currency. It does not authorise a value-bearing asset, final monetary design, public faucet, public endpoint, final fee schedule, production capacity claim or commercial launch.

For that reason, published product language should always state that prices are indicative and subject to testnet evidence. No public sale, token pricing or current production offer should be implied.
